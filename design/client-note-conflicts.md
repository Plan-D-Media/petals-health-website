# Note to Petals Health — what we are doing and why

## No brand guidelines document

We did not find a brand guidelines document with the project files, only the two fonts
and the Canva design. We have treated the Canva design itself as the definition of your
brand. If a guidelines document does exist, please send it and we will check the site
against it.

## We are tidying up near-identical colours

The design uses three slightly different dark blues and three slightly different reds. On
screen they look the same, but a website needs one exact value for each so every heading,
link and menu matches. We have settled on one blue (`#00427e`, the most-used) and one red
(`#ed1941`, the crimson used on the big headlines). The two other reds appear only on
headline accents and "Learn more" links, which is the same job, so they merge into the
crimson. For orange we kept the strong amber from the logo and the IVF buttons rather than
the pale tint.

The orange "Call 9147 405 955" buttons are a picture pasted into the design rather than a
coloured shape, so the design has no exact colour value for them. We sampled the picture
and will rebuild the button as a real gradient from `#ff8229` to `#ff4b1d`, which will look
the same but stay sharp at every screen size.

Several colours in the design fall below the readability standard for text on screen, so we
have made the red used for small links and sub-headings a little darker, and we are proposing
a slightly darker version of the orange button gradient so the white button text stays
readable. The hues do not change, only the depth.

The colours from the "I'm not a robot" badge in the mock-ups are not part of your brand
and are being ignored.

## Two fonts in the design can only be used inside Canva

The italic used for the patient quotes and the serif used for the initials in the round
avatar badges are both fonts that Canva licenses for use inside Canva only, so they cannot be
used on a website. The quotes will be set in the main brand font without italics, and the
avatar initials will use a free serif that is a close match; we will show you the match
before it goes live. Nothing else in the design is affected.

We have also made the spacing of the small capital-letter labels above section headings
consistent; in the design one of them was noticeably tighter than the others.

## One thing to confirm: the orange card

On the home page, the first of the three "How we care for your family" cards (For Her Health)
is orange while the other two are pale blue. We are building it as a permanently orange card,
not as a highlight that moves when a visitor clicks. **Please confirm the first card is meant
to stay orange.** Two readability changes on that card: the pale amber corner of the orange
has been deepened slightly so the white title stays legible, and the card's smaller text (the
line under the title and the treatment tags) is set in the brand navy instead of white so it can
be read against the orange.

## Four small labels are being made slightly bigger

The footer text, the small labels under the statistics, the doctor's speciality under
their name, and the menu links would come out between 10.7 and 12 pixels tall on screen,
which is hard to read. We are raising all four to 13 pixels, an increase of one to two
pixels. Nothing else changes size.

## Two text styles are still being measured

The small capital-letter labels above section headings (for example "PATIENT STORIES") and
the testimonial quote text are being measured from the design now, before those sections
are built.
